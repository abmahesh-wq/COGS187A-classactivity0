Personalized Information Architecture

Activity 0 showed that user knowledge changes an AI-generated design. Now ask a harder IA question: if different users have different goals, should the hierarchy and navigation adapt to them?

Big question

You are designing one university photography club website with the same underlying content. Your task is to decide how the information hierarchy should adapt for three different users—without losing usability, consistency, or predictability.

Meet the users

Maya · Beginner

First-year student. Complete beginner. Uses only an iPhone. Wants to meet people and feel comfortable joining.

Daniel · Experienced

Experienced photographer. Owns a mirrorless camera. Interested in advanced workshops, competitions, and showcasing work.

Sam · Returning member

Busy graduate student. Has attended before. Mainly wants to see upcoming events and RSVP quickly.

Content inventory

Join the club · Upcoming events · Beginner guide · Phone photography · Advanced workshops · Competitions · Member gallery · Equipment resources · Social events · About · Contact

Part 1 · Personalize the IA

  1. For each user, choose what should receive the highest homepage priority.
  2. Decide the user's main call to action.
  3. Discuss what should stay consistent across users and what can adapt.
  4. Focus on hierarchy, navigation, and task completion—not colors or visual style.

Discussion after submission

  • If Maya and Daniel see different navigation, what happens to learnability and consistency?
  • How much adaptation can happen before users lose their mental model of the site?
  • Should users know why their interface looks different?
  • Should users be able to reset or disable personalization?
  • Does personalized IA improve usability—or just move complexity into the system?

Takeaway

Traditional IA asks: How should we organize the website?

Personalized IA adds: What should remain stable, and what may reorganize itself for each user?
